Decision Lens — Six Frameworks

The financial model tells you the cost of each choice. These six frameworks help you understand the stakes. Jordan worked through each one before deciding. Use the reflection prompts to build your own picture.

🛣️
The Runway Check
How much runway does your current path actually have? Is the constraint structural or just slow?
Jordan's take
I've been at Cloudware four years. The Director track is real, but three peers were passed over in the same cycle and none left. The evidence isn't that I can't get there — it's that the path runs through two people who aren't going anywhere, on a timeline measured in years I'm not sure I want to spend waiting.
Reflect
What specific evidence tells you your current path is blocked — not just slow? Is this structural (org, headcount, politics) or performance-based? Have the doors actually been tried?
🪜
The Ladder or the Rope
Are you climbing the same ladder faster, or swinging to a different structure entirely?
Jordan's take
This is a track switch as much as anything. Cloudware PM goes toward VP Product in a scaled, process-heavy org. Meridian is building from scratch inside an Engineering org. Same title, fundamentally different game. I'm not moving up — I'm moving sideways onto a steeper slope.
Reflect
Is this move a promotion, a lateral, or a track switch? Of those three, which matters most to you at this stage? Does the new role open doors the current one can't?
📍
The Zip Code Test
What neighborhood of organizational power does each function live in?
Jordan's take
Cloudware's PM org reports to the CPO who reports to the CEO — strong zip code. But I've watched Engineering-embedded PMs get more roadmap leverage over the last two years. Meridian puts me in that neighborhood from day one. The access is different, and that difference compounds over time.
Reflect
Where does each role sit in the org chart? Does that function control budget, roadmap, or executive access? Is the new zip code actually better — or just different?
🤔
The Reluctant Yes
Did you go in hoping it would make the decision easier — and it didn't?
Jordan's take
I turned down Meridian's first outreach eight months ago. Took this one because a friend vouched for the CEO. Both final rounds ran 45 minutes over. At some point I stopped preparing and started just talking. That doesn't usually happen when I'm interviewing out of obligation.
Reflect
What was the best conversation you had in this process? Did you actively try to talk yourself out of wanting the role? What happened when you did?
👻
The Ghost Test
Not "what do you want" — what would haunt you?
Jordan's take
I keep imagining being 55 and telling someone I worked at a cloud infrastructure company through the entire fintech payments wave. That I understood it, had a real offer from inside it — and chose safety instead. That's the image I want to avoid. Not the financial outcome. The story.
Reflect
In ten years, which choice are you more likely to still be thinking about? Not which outcome is better — which path, if not taken, would sit with you?
🧱
The Floor Check
If this goes wrong, what does your floor look like?
Jordan's take
If Meridian is a disaster and I leave in 18 months, I have "Sr. PM, Payments, VC-backed fintech" on my resume. That's a more marketable credential than where I started. The floor is higher than my current position. The downside is uncomfortable — not catastrophic.
Reflect
If you take the new role and it doesn't work out in two years, what does your resume look like? Is the downside catastrophic, uncomfortable, or actually a step forward regardless?
